## Seachem Flourite Black Sand Review: The Substrate That Lasts Forever

If you've ever had aqua soil turn to mud after 18 months, the idea of a substrate that never needs replacing sounds pretty appealing. That's the pitch with Seachem Flourite Black Sand — a porous clay substrate that's good for the life of your aquarium.

I've been running it in my 90-litre community tank for about six months now. Here's whether the reality matches the promise.

## What It Actually Is

Flourite Black Sand is a specially fracted stable porous clay gravel. It's not sand in the traditional sense — the grain size is finer than regular Flourite but coarser than pool filter sand. Think somewhere between 1-2mm. It's a natural product, not coated or chemically treated, which means it won't break down over time or alter your water chemistry.

It comes in 7kg bags, which covers roughly 3-4cm depth in a 60cm tank. For a proper planted tank depth of 5-7cm, you'll likely need two bags for anything over 60 litres.

## The Rinsing Situation

Let's get this out of the way — you need to rinse Flourite Black Sand thoroughly before use. And I mean thoroughly. I spent a good 30 minutes rinsing mine in a bucket with a garden hose and the water was still running slightly cloudy. If you skip this step or half-arse it, your tank will look like a mud puddle for days.

Even after rinsing, expect some cloudiness when you first fill the tank. It settles within 24-48 hours with a filter running. Don't panic — this is normal and it clears up completely.

## Plant Growth

Here's the thing about Flourite — it's inert. Unlike Fluval Stratum or ADA Amazonia, it contains essentially zero nutrients out of the bag. Your plants won't starve on it, but they won't thrive without supplementation either.

What it does offer is a porous structure with decent cation exchange capacity (CEC). That means it can absorb and hold nutrients from the water column and root tabs, making them available to plant roots over time. Think of it as a nutrient sponge rather than a nutrient source.

After six months with root tabs every 3-4 months:

- **Cryptocoryne wendtii** — lush and full, loves the fine grain for rooting
- **Amazon sword** — solid growth with regular root tab feeding
- **Vallisneria** — runners everywhere, no issues
- **Java fern on driftwood** — thriving (doesn't use substrate anyway)
- **Dwarf sag** — slow but steady spread

Heavy root feeders like swords absolutely need root tabs in Flourite. Without them, they'll stall and yellow within a couple of months. Budget about $15-20 AUD per year on root tabs.

## What I Liked

- **Lasts forever.** Six months in and the grain structure is identical to day one. No breakdown, no mushiness, no compaction. Seachem says "life of the aquarium" and I believe it.
- **Looks great.** The black colour makes fish and plant colours pop. It's a more natural look than the uniform black of ADA Amazonia.
- **Doesn't buffer pH.** If you want neutral water chemistry without your substrate fighting you, Flourite delivers. My pH has stayed rock steady.
- **Fine grain.** Corydoras and kuhli loaches can sift through it without damaging their barbels — important if you keep bottom dwellers.
- **No ammonia spike.** Unlike aqua soils, you can add fish much sooner after setup. No weeks of waiting for the ammonia leach to clear.

## What I Didn't Like

- **No nutrients.** You're buying a housing for nutrients, not nutrients themselves. Root tabs are mandatory for anything beyond java fern and anubias.
- **Rinsing is tedious.** Plan for 20-30 minutes of rinsing per bag. Skip it and you'll regret it for days.
- **Price.** At ~$89 AUD for 7kg, it's not cheap for what is essentially fancy clay. You'll need two bags for most tanks, so you're looking at $180 before you even add root tabs.
- **Dust settles on leaves.** Even after rinsing, fine clay particles settle on plant leaves for the first week. Annoying but temporary.
- **Heavy.** Noticeably heavier than Stratum or Amazonia. Not a problem once it's in the tank, but worth knowing if you're carrying bags upstairs.

## Flourite vs Aqua Soil — Which Approach?

It comes down to what you value:

**Choose Flourite if:** you want a substrate you never replace, you keep bottom dwellers that need fine grain, you don't want pH buffering, or you're running a low-tech setup where you'd supplement with root tabs anyway.

**Choose aqua soil if:** you want immediate plant growth without supplementation, you're doing a high-tech setup with CO2, or you don't mind replacing substrate every 18-24 months when it breaks down.

I've run both. Flourite is less exciting up front but wins long-term on maintenance and cost if you factor in not replacing it every couple of years.

## Who Should Buy This?

Flourite Black Sand is the right substrate if you're setting up a long-term planted tank and don't want to think about substrate replacement. It's ideal for low-to-medium tech setups with root tab supplementation, and it's the best option for tanks with corydoras or other bottom dwellers that need a fine, smooth substrate.

If you want maximum plant growth from day one without root tabs, look at Fluval Stratum instead — it costs less and comes with nutrients built in, but it'll need replacing in 18 months.

## Final Verdict — 4.1/5

Seachem Flourite Black Sand is a buy-it-once substrate that does exactly what it promises — provide a stable, porous, long-lasting base for planted tanks. It won't wow you with explosive plant growth out of the bag, but paired with root tabs it grows everything I've thrown at it. The black colour looks stunning, bottom dwellers love it, and it'll outlast every other substrate in the hobby.

The price and the rinsing are the main drawbacks. If you can get past those, it's a genuinely solid long-term investment.
