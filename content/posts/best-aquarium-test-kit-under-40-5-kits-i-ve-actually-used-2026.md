I've spent the last eight months testing five of the most popular kits available in Australia, running them side-by-side on the same water samples from my 200-litre planted community tank and my 90-litre shrimp breeding setup. Here's what I found.

## What to Look For in an Aquarium Test Kit Under $40

Before you pick a kit, here's what actually matters:

### Accuracy

Liquid reagent kits are more accurate than test strips. Period. The colour differentiation is sharper, the chemistry is more controlled, and you can repeat tests more reliably. If your budget allows one kit, make it liquid.

### Parameters Covered

At minimum, you need ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH. These are the "big four" that indicate whether your nitrogen cycle is working and your water is safe. GH and KH are important for species-specific setups (shrimp, African cichlids, discus) but less critical for a standard community tank.

### Cost Per Test

Don't just look at the sticker price. A $35 kit with 800 tests is dramatically cheaper per test than a $15 tube of 25 strips. Over a year of twice-weekly testing, you'll spend roughly $3.60 on the liquid kit versus $36+ replacing strip tubes.

### Shelf Life and Reagent Stability

Liquid reagents last 18+ months once opened if stored properly (cool, dark, caps tight). Strips degrade faster once the tube is opened, especially in humid environments — and if you're in Queensland or the Northern Territory, humidity is a real factor.

### Availability in Australia

Some brands are easier to source locally than others. API and Tetra are stocked at every major pet retailer. NT Labs and Sera may require ordering from specialist shops or online retailers. Factor in shipping costs and wait times when comparing prices.
