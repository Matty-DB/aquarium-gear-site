## Why the AquaClear 30 Is the Budget Filter to Beat

Look, I've run a lot of filters over the years. Cheap ones that rattled themselves apart in weeks, overpriced ones that didn't justify the sticker, and everything in between. If a mate asked me tomorrow what filter to grab for their new tank without spending a fortune, I wouldn't even hesitate — it's the AquaClear 30, every time.

I've had one running on my 75-litre community tank for over six months now, and honestly, most days I forget it's there. The water stays clear, the fish are happy, and the thing just quietly does its job. That's all you really want from a filter.

## What Makes It Stand Out

The AquaClear 30 is a hang-on-back (HOB) filter made by Fluval, and its main trick is the three-stage media basket. Instead of those disposable cartridges that half the industry tries to lock you into — which are a racket, let's be honest — you get three separate components: a foam block for mechanical filtration, activated carbon in a mesh bag, and Fluval's BioMax ceramic rings for biological filtration.

Each piece comes out independently, so you can rinse, swap, or replace them on their own schedule. After about three months, I ditched the carbon altogether and dropped in a second bag of BioMax. That flexibility is the entire point, and it's why the ongoing costs are basically nothing. A bag of generic ceramic rings runs $8–12 AUD and lasts years.

The flow rate tops out at 568 litres per hour, which gives you solid turnover on tanks up to about 114 litres. There's a simple lever on the intake tube that lets you dial the flow right down — handy if you're keeping bettas or anything else that doesn't appreciate being blown around the tank like a leaf in a storm.

## My Experience After Six Months

The ceramic impeller needs about 24 hours to bed in when it's brand new. During that first day, there's a faint rattle that might concern you — don't stress, it sorts itself out. After that break-in period, it runs near-silent. Mine sits in the lounge room and nobody in the house has ever complained about noise.

Water clarity has been consistently excellent. I'm running a mixed community — tetras, corydoras, a bristlenose — and the AquaClear handles the bioload without breaking a sweat. I do fortnightly water changes of about 30%, and the foam block gets a quick squeeze in old tank water once a month. That's it for maintenance.

The filter is widely stocked at Petbarn, Aquotix, and most independent fish shops across Australia. You won't be hunting for it online or waiting three weeks for shipping from overseas. Typically runs **$40–68 AUD depending on the retailer**, which puts it comfortably in budget territory.

## The Downsides (Being Honest)

No filter is perfect, and the AquaClear 30 has a few quirks worth knowing about.

**No self-priming.** Every time you restart it after a water change, you need to manually fill the filter box with tank water before it'll kick in. It means wet hands and a cup or jug nearby. It's mildly annoying, not a dealbreaker, but if that sounds like a hassle you'd genuinely hate, look into the Seachem Tidal 35 instead.

**Fixed intake tube length.** The intake tube is roughly 22 cm and can't be shortened without cutting it. On smaller or shallower tanks, this can limit where you position it.

**Lid doesn't seal perfectly.** Over months, micro-splashing leaves mineral deposits along the rim. It's cosmetic and easy to wipe off, but worth mentioning.

## Who Should Buy This Filter

The AquaClear 30 is ideal if you're running a freshwater community tank in the 40–110 litre range and you want something reliable that won't nickel-and-dime you on replacement media. It suits beginners who want a set-and-forget option and experienced keepers who appreciate the media flexibility.

It's not the right pick for nano tanks under 30 litres (too much flow, even dialled down), shrimp-only setups (a sponge filter is safer for shrimplets), or anyone who absolutely needs self-priming convenience.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.7/5**

The AquaClear 30 isn't flashy. It doesn't have app connectivity, LED indicators, or any of the bells and whistles that some newer filters are tacking on. What it does have is a decade-plus track record of just working, the lowest ongoing media costs of any filter in this price bracket, and the kind of build quality that means you'll probably still be running it five years from now.

For most fishkeepers on a budget, it's the safest recommendation I can make. Grab one, set it up, and get back to enjoying your tank.
