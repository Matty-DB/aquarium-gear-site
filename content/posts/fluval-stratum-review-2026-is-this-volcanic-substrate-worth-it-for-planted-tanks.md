## Fluval Stratum Review 2026: My Honest Take After Eight Months

If you've been researching substrates for a planted aquarium or shrimp tank, you've almost certainly come across Fluval Stratum. It's one of the most recommended substrates in the hobby — but is it actually worth the money, especially here in Australia?

I've been running Fluval Stratum in two tanks: a 60-litre planted nano for about eight months and a dedicated Crystal Red Shrimp breeding setup for the past five months. Here's everything I've learnt — the good, the frustrating, and whether it deserves that reputation.

## What Is Fluval Stratum?

Fluval Stratum is a lightweight volcanic soil substrate sourced from the foothills of Mount Aso in Japan. It comes as irregularly shaped, dark granules roughly 2–4 mm in diameter. The granules are porous, giving beneficial bacteria loads of surface area to colonise — a genuine advantage for biological filtration.

Unlike inert substrates such as plain gravel or sand, Stratum actively interacts with your water chemistry. It buffers pH downward (typically landing between 6.0 and 6.8 depending on your tap water) and provides a mildly acidic, soft-water environment. That makes it particularly popular for Caridina shrimp and plants that favour slightly acidic conditions.

### Key Specs

- **Material:** Volcanic soil (Mount Aso, Japan)
- **Available sizes:** 2 kg, 4 kg, 8 kg bags
- **Grain size:** Approximately 2–4 mm, irregular
- **pH buffering:** Lowers to approximately 6.0–6.8
- **Colour:** Dark brown to black when wet
- **Recommended depth:** 5–8 cm for planted tanks
- **AU Price:** Roughly $35–45 AUD for an 8 kg bag at Petbarn or your local aquarium shop

## Setting It Up: The Dust Is Real

Let's get the biggest gripe out of the way first. When I added Stratum to my 60-litre tank, the water turned into chocolate milk. Fluval says not to rinse it, and honestly, rinsing doesn't help much — the granules are so soft that washing them just creates more dust.

My approach was to fill the tank extremely slowly using a colander placed over the substrate, letting the water trickle in over about 45 minutes. Even with that method, the water was murky for a solid 36 hours before my hang-on-back filter (running filter floss as a polishing pad) cleared it up. In my shrimp tank with only a sponge filter, it took closer to three days.

If you're planning a scape and want to see results immediately, budget for at least two to three days of patience.

## Planting Experience

Once settled, Stratum is genuinely pleasant to plant in. The granules are light enough that you can push stem plants in with tweezers without much resistance, and they hold roots reasonably well once established. My Rotala rotundifolia and Ludwigia repens anchored within about a week.

Smaller foreground plants like Monte Carlo were a different story. The lightweight granules meant tiny plantlets kept floating up for the first fortnight. I ended up using small stainless steel mesh to pin them down until the roots took hold. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if you're planning a carpet.

Cryptocoryne wendtii, on the other hand, absolutely thrived. The roots spread through the Stratum easily, and I noticed noticeably faster growth compared to inert substrates during the first three months.

## Nutrient Content: Set Realistic Expectations

Fluval markets Stratum as containing "mineral-rich" volcanic nutrients, and that's technically true — but the nutrient content is modest compared to dedicated aqua soils.

In my planted nano, I started seeing slower growth in demanding plants around the five-month mark. My Alternanthera reineckii, which had been putting out vibrant red leaves, started looking washed out. Adding root tabs (I use Osmocote+ capsules, about $12 AUD for a year's supply) brought the colour back within two weeks.

For a low-tech setup with undemanding plants — java fern, anubias, cryptocorynes, and mosses — the base nutrients are probably sufficient for 8–12 months. For anything medium-tech or above, plan on supplementing with root tabs from month four or five onwards.

## pH Buffering: The Standout Feature

The pH buffering is Stratum's genuine standout feature, especially for shrimp keepers. My tap water here in Melbourne sits around 7.2–7.4, and Stratum pulled it down to a stable 6.4 in both tanks within the first week.

After eight months in the planted nano, the buffering has weakened slightly — I'm now reading 6.6–6.8. Based on reports from other hobbyists and my own trajectory, the buffering capacity lasts roughly 12–18 months before it becomes essentially inert. That's fairly standard for volcanic soil substrates.

## Shrimp Keepers: This Is Where It Shines

This is where Stratum really earns its reputation. My Crystal Red Shrimp colony has been thriving on it. The combination of stable acidic pH, soft granules that don't damage delicate shrimp legs, and the porous surface that grows biofilm (which shrimplets graze on constantly) makes it close to ideal.

The dark colour also makes the shrimp pop visually — my CRS look absolutely stunning against the near-black substrate.

For Neocaridina species, Stratum works perfectly fine too, though it's arguably overkill since Neos are less fussy about pH. If you're keeping Cherry Shrimp in a community tank, you could save some money with an inert substrate and not notice much difference.

## Granule Longevity

The granules do break down over time. After eight months, I'd estimate 10–15% of the top layer has crumbled into finer sediment. This isn't catastrophic, but it does mean the substrate compacts slightly. During water changes, I'm more careful with the siphon now — aggressive gravel vacuuming will suck up the broken-down particles and make a mess.

I wouldn't plan on reusing Stratum after a full tear-down. After 18–24 months, most of the granules will have degraded enough that the substrate is essentially spent. Factor this in as a recurring cost — at $35–45 AUD per 8 kg bag, it adds up for larger tanks.

## Pros and Cons

### Pros
- Buffers pH to 6.0–6.8, ideal for Caridina shrimp and acid-loving plants
- Lightweight granules make planting with aquascaping tweezers effortless
- Porous surface promotes strong beneficial bacteria colonisation
- Dark colour enhances fish and shrimp colouration
- Widely available in Australia — stocked at most Petbarn locations and independent aquarium shops
- Minimal ammonia leaching, allowing faster cycling (livestock safe within 10–14 days)

### Cons
- Severe initial cloudiness lasting 36–72 hours
- Granules break down over 12–18 months, requiring full replacement
- Nutrient content is moderate — demanding plants need root tab supplementation from month four
- Lightweight granules make anchoring carpeting plants frustrating without mesh or weights
- Cannot be effectively gravel-vacuumed without sucking up degraded fine particles

## Final Verdict: 4.3 out of 5

After eight months of daily use across two tanks, my Fluval Stratum review for 2026 comes down to this: it's a genuinely good substrate that excels in a specific niche.

The sweet spot is shrimp keepers and low-to-medium tech planted tanks. The pH buffering is reliable, the planting experience is pleasant, and the biological filtration benefits are real. Just go in with realistic expectations about nutrient longevity and granule degradation.

If you're running a Caridina shrimp tank or a simple planted setup, this is an excellent choice that's easy to find at any Petbarn in Australia. Grab an 8 kg bag, pick up a packet of root tabs for down the track, and you're sorted.
