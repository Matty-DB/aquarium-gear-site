## What to Look For in an Aquarium Water Pump Under $40

Before you pick a pump, it's worth understanding what actually matters at this price point. Here's what I consider when testing budget aquarium water pumps.

### Flow Rate vs. Your Tank Size

The general rule is to turn over your tank volume 4-6 times per hour for a tropical community setup, and 6-10 times for high-flow species or planted tanks with CO2. A 100-litre tank needs a pump rated at 400-600 L/h minimum.

But here's the catch: manufacturers rate flow at zero head height. The moment you add vertical lift (pumping water up to a spray bar or through a sump return), actual flow drops significantly. A pump rated at 1000 L/h might deliver only 600 L/h at 0.5 metres of head. Always buy more flow than you think you need.

### Head Height

Head height tells you how high the pump can push water vertically before flow drops to zero. For a standard tank with an internal filter or spray bar, 0.5-0.8 metres is usually fine. For sump returns, measure the distance from the sump water level to the tank rim and add 20% as a buffer.

### Noise

This is where cheap pumps often fall apart — literally. A noisy pump usually means the impeller is vibrating against the housing, which causes wear and eventually failure. Magnetic impeller designs (like the Hydor and Hailea on this list) tend to run quieter than direct-drive models because there's no physical contact between the motor and the impeller.

If silence matters — bedroom tanks, open-plan living areas — spend the extra $10-15 for a pump with proper magnetic coupling.

### Impeller Material

Ceramic impellers and shafts last significantly longer than plastic ones. At the sub-$40 price point, you'll find both. Ceramic resists wear from sand and fine debris, while plastic impellers develop play in the shaft housing after 12-18 months and start making noise. If you're running a sand substrate, ceramic is non-negotiable.

### Power Consumption

A pump running 24/7 uses more electricity than you might expect over a year. The difference between a 6-watt and a 10-watt pump is about $10-12 AUD per year at average Australian electricity rates (~$0.30/kWh). Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting if you're running multiple tanks.

### Australian Availability

This might sound obvious, but it matters more than you'd think. Pumps from Australian-distributed brands (Aqua One, Hailea) are easy to warranty and replace. Pumps you order directly from overseas sellers on AliExpress might save $5 upfront, but if the impeller dies in month two, you're buying a whole new pump rather than a $10 replacement part.
