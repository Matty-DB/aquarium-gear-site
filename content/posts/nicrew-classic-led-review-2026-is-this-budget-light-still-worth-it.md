## NICREW ClassicLED Plus Review 2026: The Budget Light That Keeps Delivering

If you've spent any time searching for affordable aquarium lighting in Australia, you've almost certainly come across the NICREW ClassicLED Plus. It's been a staple recommendation on fishkeeping forums for years, and heading into 2026, it's still one of the most popular lights for beginners and budget-conscious hobbyists.

I've been running the NICREW ClassicLED Plus on my 120-litre community tank for over six months now. Here's my honest take on whether this light deserves its reputation — and whether it's the right pick for your setup.

## What You Get in the Box

The packaging is no-frills. Inside you'll find the light unit, a pair of adjustable extendable brackets (clear plastic with rubber grips), and a power adapter. No timer, no remote, no app — just a single-page quick start sheet.

The brackets are the clever bit. They're telescoping plastic arms that slide in and out, letting you fit the light across a range of tank widths. My 76 cm model adjusts from roughly 76 to 107 cm, which sat perfectly on my 90 cm tank without any fuss. The rubber grip pads on the bracket ends do a decent job keeping the light from sliding around.

The housing is slim aluminium — about 1.5 cm thick — which acts as a passive heatsink. After six months of daily use (roughly 8 hours a day on a separate timer), the aluminium gets warm to the touch but never hot. That's a good sign for LED longevity.

## Build Quality and Design

Let's be honest: this is a $40–$55 AUD light. You're not getting premium materials. The aluminium housing feels solid enough, but the plastic end caps and brackets are the weak points. One of my brackets developed a hairline crack after about four months — it still works, but it's worth mentioning.

The power cord runs about 1.5 metres, which is adequate for most setups but could be tight if your power outlet isn't close to the tank. There's a small inline switch box with two toggles: one for white LEDs and one for blue. You can run white only, blue only, or both together. Simple, reliable, and nothing to crash or disconnect.

What I genuinely appreciate is how thin this light looks when mounted. At only about 1.5 cm in profile, it sits low and clean on top of the tank. More than one visitor has assumed it cost far more than it did.

## Light Performance

This is where the ClassicLED Plus earns its keep. Compared to the standard Classic model, the Plus adds RGB LEDs that noticeably improve colour rendering. Fish colours look richer and more natural — my cardinal tetras and cherry barbs really pop under this light.

The white LEDs sit at roughly 7,000K colour temperature, giving a clean daylight appearance. Total output is around 1,200 lumens on the 76 cm model, which is a meaningful step up from the base Classic's 920 lumens.

The blue moonlight mode is a nice touch for evening viewing. I run mine for about 30 minutes before lights-out to give the fish a transition period, and it creates a really pleasant ambient glow in the room.

In my testing with a borrowed PAR meter, the ClassicLED Plus delivered about 50–55 PAR at 30 cm depth in the centre of the tank. That's comfortably in the low-to-medium light range, opening up a much wider selection of plants than the base model.

## Plant Growth: What Can You Actually Grow?

After six months, here's what's thrived under the ClassicLED Plus in my tank:

- **Java Fern** — steady growth, no algae issues on leaves
- **Anubias Nana** — slow but healthy, produced two new rhizome shoots
- **Java Moss** — grows like a weed, needs trimming every few weeks
- **Cryptocoryne Wendtii** — took a month to adjust, then filled in beautifully
- **Ludwigia Repens** — good colour and steady upward growth
- **Amazon Sword** — established well, throwing out new leaves regularly

What still struggled:

- **Dwarf Hairgrass** — grew slowly and stayed patchy, even with root tabs
- **Monte Carlo** — never really took off without CO2

The verdict: the ClassicLED Plus handles low-light plants with ease and does a respectable job with medium-light species too. If you want a full carpet or demanding high-light plants, you'll need to step up to something like the Fluval Plant 3.0 — but for the price, this light covers a lot of ground.

## Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
- Aluminium housing acts as an effective passive heatsink — stays warm, never hot
- Extendable brackets adjust 76–107 cm (on the 76 cm model) with rubber grip pads
- Dual switch for independent white and blue LED control
- RGB LEDs deliver noticeably better colour rendering than the base Classic
- ~50–55 PAR at 30 cm depth supports low-to-medium light plants
- Ultra-slim 1.5 cm profile looks clean on any tank
- Very affordable at $40–$55 AUD

**Cons:**
- Plastic bracket end caps prone to cracking — one of mine cracked at the four-month mark
- No built-in timer — you'll need a $8–$10 mechanical timer from Bunnings
- Power cord only 1.5 metres, which limits placement options
- Not enough output for high-light or demanding carpeting plants
- Splash-resistant only — not submersible, so condensation is a long-term concern
- RGB mode can look a bit garish at full intensity — needs balancing via the switch

## Buying in Australia

The NICREW ClassicLED Plus is readily available through Amazon Australia and turns up on eBay AU regularly as well. Petbarn and the major pet chains don't tend to carry NICREW — it's predominantly an online brand here.

Shipping from Amazon AU is typically 2–5 business days to east coast metro areas. If you're in a regional area, factor in a few extra days. Pricing generally sits around $40–$55 AUD, though I've seen it drop to around $38 during sales events.

One thing to note: warranty claims on NICREW products in Australia go through the Amazon seller, not NICREW directly. I've heard mixed reports on the process, so keep your receipt and packaging for the first few months just in case.

## Final Verdict — 4.2/5

The NICREW ClassicLED Plus is the best value aquarium light under $60 AUD in Australia for 2026. It's not going to win awards for build quality or features, but it delivers where it matters: solid light output, decent colour rendering, and enough PAR to grow a genuine range of live plants.

If you're setting up a new community tank, running a low-tech planted setup, or just need a reliable light for a secondary tank without spending a fortune, the ClassicLED Plus is a smart buy. The small premium over the base Classic model is absolutely worth it for the improved output and RGB colour enhancement.

The plastic brackets remain the weak point, and you'll need to budget an extra $8–$10 for a timer plug, but those are minor quibbles for a light at this price. Six months in, mine is still going strong — and I have no plans to replace it.
