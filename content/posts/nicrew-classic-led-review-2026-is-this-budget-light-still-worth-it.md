## NICREW Classic LED Review 2026: The Budget Light That Refuses to Die

If you've spent any time searching for affordable aquarium lighting, you've almost certainly stumbled across the NICREW Classic LED. It's been a staple recommendation in fishkeeping forums for years now, and heading into 2026, it's still one of the most frequently purchased lights for beginners and budget-conscious hobbyists alike.

But does cheap actually mean good value? I've been running the NICREW Classic LED on my 120-litre community tank for over six months now, and I've also tested several competing lights in the same price bracket. Here's my honest take on whether this light deserves its reputation — or whether there are better options for Australian fishkeepers in 2026.

## What You Get in the Box

The NICREW Classic LED ships in a pretty no-frills package. Inside you'll find the light unit itself, a pair of adjustable extendable brackets (clear plastic with rubber grips), and a basic power adapter. There's no timer, no remote, and no instruction manual beyond a single-page quick start sheet.

The brackets are the clever bit here. They're telescoping plastic arms that slide in and out, letting you fit the light across a range of tank widths. My 76 cm model adjusts from roughly 76 to 107 cm, which meant it sat perfectly on my 90 cm tank without any modifications. The rubber grip pads on the bracket ends actually do a decent job of keeping the light from sliding — something I can't say for every budget light I've tested.

The housing is slim aluminium — about 1.5 cm thick — which acts as a passive heatsink. After six months of daily use (roughly 8 hours a day on a timer I added separately), the aluminium gets warm to the touch but never hot. That's a good sign for LED longevity.

## Build Quality and Design

Let's be real: this is a $30–$45 AUD light. You're not getting premium materials. The aluminium housing feels solid enough, but the plastic end caps and brackets are clearly the weak points. I had one bracket develop a hairline crack after about four months — it still works fine, but it's worth noting.

The power cord is about 1.5 metres long, which is adequate for most setups but might be tight if your power outlet isn't near your tank. There's a small inline switch box with two toggles: one for white LEDs and one for blue LEDs. You can run white only, blue only, or both together. It's simple and it works — no app to crash, no Bluetooth to disconnect.

One thing I genuinely appreciate is how thin and unobtrusive this light is when mounted. At only about 1.5 cm in profile, it sits low and clean on top of the tank. Visitors to my place have commented that it looks much more expensive than it is.

## Light Performance: How Bright Is It Really?

This is where the NICREW Classic LED earns its keep. For a budget light, the output is genuinely impressive. The white LEDs run at roughly 6,500K colour temperature, which gives a clean, slightly cool daylight appearance. Fish colours pop nicely under it — my cardinal tetras and cherry barbs look noticeably more vibrant than they did under my old fluorescent tube.

The blue moonlight mode is a nice touch for evening viewing. It's not just a gimmick — I run mine for about 30 minutes before lights-out to give the fish a transition period, and it creates a really pleasant ambient glow in the room.

In my testing with a PAR meter (borrowed from a mate in the planted tank community), the 76 cm NICREW Classic LED delivered roughly 35–40 PAR at 30 cm depth in the centre of the tank. That's enough for low-light plants like Java fern, Anubias, and various mosses. If you're trying to grow anything more demanding — stem plants, carpeting species — you'll need to step up to the Classic LED Plus or a different light altogether.

## Plant Growth: What Can You Actually Grow?

After six months, here's what's thrived under the NICREW Classic LED in my tank:

- **Java Fern** — steady growth, no algae issues on leaves
- **Anubias Nana** — slow but healthy, produced two new rhizome shoots
- **Java Moss** — grows like a weed, needs trimming every few weeks
- **Cryptocoryne Wendtii** — took a month to adjust, then filled in nicely

And what struggled:

- **Dwarf Hairgrass** — barely grew, stayed patchy despite root tabs
- **Rotala Rotundifolia** — grew leggy and stretched toward the light, never bushed out

The verdict on plants is clear: the NICREW Classic LED is a low-light fixture. It's perfect if you're running easy, beginner-friendly plants. If you want a planted tank with any real ambition, look at the Plus model or budget lights with higher PAR output.

In my testing, the Plus delivered about 50–55 PAR at 30 cm depth — a meaningful jump that opens up medium-light plants like Ludwigia and Amazon swords. The colour rendering is also warmer and more natural, which makes both fish and plants look better.

The build quality is essentially identical — same aluminium housing, same bracket system, same inline switch box. You're paying the premium purely for better LEDs, and in my view, that's money well spent if you have any interest in live plants.

## What to Look For in a Budget Aquarium Light

Before you click "add to cart" on any budget LED, here are the criteria I use when evaluating lights in this price range:

### PAR Output, Not Just Lumens

Lumens tell you how bright a light appears to the human eye. PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation) tells you how much usable light your plants actually receive. A light can look bright but deliver poor PAR. For low-light plants, you want at least 30 PAR at your substrate depth. For medium-light, aim for 50+.

### Colour Temperature

Look for lights in the 6,000K–7,500K range. Below 6,000K and your tank looks yellow and washed out. Above 8,000K and it looks clinical and cold. The sweet spot for most community tanks is around 6,500K — which is exactly where the NICREW Classic sits.

### Adjustable Brackets

Make sure the light fits your tank. Measure your tank's rim-to-rim width in centimetres and check the bracket extension range. Nothing's more annoying than a light that doesn't quite reach both sides.

### Heat Management

Aluminium housings dissipate heat far better than plastic ones. Heat is the enemy of LED longevity. If two lights are the same price and one has an aluminium body, pick that one every time.

### Timer Compatibility

Almost no budget light includes a built-in timer, so plan to buy a separate mechanical or smart timer plug. Consistent photoperiod (8–10 hours for most tanks) is critical for plant health and algae prevention. A basic mechanical timer from Bunnings costs about $8 AUD and does the job perfectly.
