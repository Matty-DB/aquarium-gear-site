## PYPABL Aquarium LED Light Review: The Best Budget Light Under $25?

When you're setting up a tank on a budget, lighting is one of those things that's tempting to cheap out on. I've been there — bought a few $15 clip-on lights that barely grew algae, let alone plants. So when I picked up the PYPABL Full Spectrum LED for under $25 AUD on Amazon AU, I wasn't expecting much.

After running it on my 40-litre nano for four months, I'm genuinely impressed.

## What You Get

The PYPABL is a 10-watt full spectrum LED light designed for tanks between 30 and 50 cm long. It puts out around 1,500 lumens at 6500K — clean, bright daylight colour. The LED array is 33 beads: 22 white, 4 red, 3 green, and 4 blue. That WRGB mix gives you a proper full spectrum rather than the washed-out look you get from white-only budget lights.

It comes with extendable brackets that slide out about 10 cm on each side, so it fits tanks from 30 to 50 cm without any modifications. The brackets have rubber grip feet that sit on the tank rim — stable enough that I've never had it shift.

The power cable is 1.5 metres with an Australian standard plug. No adapter needed.

## The Built-In Timer Changes Everything

This is the feature that sets it apart from every other light in this price range. There's a built-in timer with four presets: 6, 9, 12, and 24 hours. Press the button, pick your duration, and it handles the rest. No separate timer plug from Bunnings, no forgetting to turn the lights off, no algae bloom from running 14 hours because you lost track.

At this price point, most lights give you a manual on/off switch and nothing else. Having a timer built in for under $25 is genuinely unusual and makes a real difference for plant health and algae prevention.

## Light Performance

I don't have exact PAR readings for this light, but based on plant growth over four months, I'd estimate it sits comfortably in the low-to-medium light range at 30 cm depth.

What's grown well:
- **Java fern** — steady, healthy growth
- **Anubias nana** — slow but no issues
- **Cryptocoryne wendtii** — filled in nicely after initial melt
- **Java moss** — needs regular trimming
- **Ludwigia repens** — decent colour and growth

What struggled:
- **Monte Carlo** — stayed patchy without CO2
- **Dwarf hairgrass** — too slow to carpet

For a low-tech setup without CO2, it covers the plants most beginners want to grow. If you're after a full carpet or demanding high-light species, you'll need to step up to something like the Fluval Plant 3.0.

## What I Liked

- **Built-in timer** — set it once, forget about it. Game changer at this price.
- **Full spectrum WRGB** — fish colours pop, plants get a proper light mix.
- **Price** — under $25 AUD. Hard to argue with that.
- **Bright enough** — 1,500 lumens is solid for tanks up to 50 cm.
- **Extendable brackets** — fits a range of tank sizes without fuss.
- **Aussie plug** — no adapter needed, works straight out of the box.

## What I Didn't Like

- **Plastic build** — it's all plastic, no aluminium heatsink. Runs a bit warm after 8+ hours but hasn't been an issue so far.
- **No dimming** — it's full brightness or off. Can't dial it down if you're getting algae.
- **Limited size range** — only fits 30-50 cm tanks. If you've got anything bigger, you'll need a different light.
- **Brand longevity unknown** — PYPABL isn't a household name. No idea if it'll last 2 years or 5. At this price, replacing it yearly wouldn't break the bank anyway.

## Who Should Buy This?

If you're setting up a nano tank or small community tank on a budget, this is the light to get. The built-in timer alone makes it worth picking over other budget options that cost the same but make you buy a separate timer.

It's also a great option for a quarantine tank, hospital tank, or any secondary setup where you don't want to spend real money on lighting.

If your tank is over 50 cm or you want to grow demanding plants, look at the Fluval Plant 3.0 instead — it's more money but it's a different league.

## Final Verdict — 4.2/5

The PYPABL Full Spectrum LED punches well above its $25 AUD price tag. The built-in timer, proper WRGB spectrum, and decent brightness make it the best value aquarium light I've tested for small tanks. It's not going to compete with lights five times its price, but it doesn't need to. For low-tech planted tanks under 50 cm, it does exactly what you need.
