## What to Look For in a Return Pump Under $100

Before you pull the trigger, here are the key factors I weigh when choosing a return pump for a sump setup:

### Flow Rate and Tank Turnover
The general rule is you want your return pump to turn over the total system volume 5–10 times per hour. For a 200-litre system, that means a pump rated between 1,000 and 2,000 L/hr. But remember — manufacturer ratings are measured at zero head height. Once you account for the vertical distance from your sump to the tank, plus every elbow and fitting in your plumbing, you'll lose 20–40% of that rated flow. Always oversize slightly.

### DC vs AC Motors
DC pumps give you speed control, run cooler, use less electricity, and are generally quieter at lower speeds. AC pumps are simpler, cheaper, and have fewer electronic components that can fail. For sump return duty under $100, I lean towards DC (the Jebao DCP-2500 is my top pick for a reason), but a quality AC pump like the Sicce Syncra Silent is equally valid if you don't need adjustability.

### Head Height
This is the maximum vertical distance the pump can push water. Your return pump needs enough head height to get water from the sump up to the display tank with pressure to spare. Measure the vertical distance from the pump's position to the return outlet, add 10–15% for friction losses in the plumbing, and make sure your pump comfortably exceeds that number.

### Noise Level
Return pumps run 24/7. A pump that seems "fine" for the first hour can become maddening after a week. DC pumps at reduced speed are generally quietest. Anti-vibration feet and rubber mounting help enormously. If your tank is anywhere you spend time, prioritise quiet operation.

### Impeller Material
Ceramic impellers and shafts outlast plastic ones significantly — typically 2–3 years versus 6–12 months in my experience. They're also quieter because ceramic is smoother and creates less friction. At this price point, most decent pumps offer ceramic, but always check.

### Power Consumption
Your return pump runs every minute of every day. The difference between a 15-watt DC pump and a 35-watt AC pump works out to roughly $25–$30 AUD per year on typical Australian electricity rates. Over the life of the pump, the cheaper-to-buy AC option might actually cost more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I clean my sump return pump?
I clean mine every 8–12 weeks. Pull the impeller assembly out, soak it in a 50/50 white vinegar and water solution for 30 minutes, then rinse thoroughly. This removes calcium and coralline algae buildup that reduces flow and increases noise.

### Can I use a return pump that's too powerful?
Yes — too much flow can overwhelm your overflow and flood your display tank's stand. If you buy a pump with more flow than you need, use a DC controller to dial it back or install a ball valve on the return line. This is actually why I prefer DC pumps — built-in adjustability.

### Do I need a backup return pump?
Honestly, yes. If your return pump dies, your sump stops filtering and your tank temperature can swing. I keep a spare Jebao DCS-2000 on hand specifically because it's cheap enough to justify as a backup. At $50–$65, it's cheap insurance.

### Are Jebao pumps reliable enough for a sump?
The newer DCP and DCS series are significantly better than Jebao's earlier models. I've run DCP pumps for over a year without issues. That said, they're not in the same reliability tier as Eheim or Sicce. For the price difference, I think they're excellent value — just keep a backup handy.
