## What to Look For When Buying a Rimless Tank Under $150

Before you pick one of these tanks, here are the key factors I weigh when evaluating a rimless aquarium at this price point:

### Glass Type and Clarity
Standard float glass has a slight green tint — most visible on the edges and at wider panels. Low-iron glass (sometimes called ultra-clear or Starphire) eliminates this. At the under-$150 level, only the UNS and Waterbox use genuine low-iron glass. The others use standard float glass, which is perfectly fine but won't give you that "invisible" look in photos.

### Silicone Quality
On a rimless tank, the silicone joints are structural — there's no plastic frame hiding sloppy workmanship. Look for thin, even beads with no bubbles or gaps. Every tank on this list has acceptable silicone, but the Dennerle and UNS are noticeably cleaner.

### Included Equipment vs. Tank Only
The Fluval Flex and Aqua One Focus come with lights and filtration built in. The Dennerle, UNS, and Waterbox are tank-only (or tank plus filter). Factor in the additional cost of a light ($50–120), filter ($30–80), and heater ($25–45) if you're buying a bare tank — that under-$150 price can balloon to $250+ quickly.

### Footprint and Placement
Rimless tanks without bracing are more fragile than framed tanks. They need a perfectly flat, level surface — no overhanging edges. I use a thin yoga mat cut to size under all my rimless tanks to cushion minor surface irregularities and dampen vibration.

### Australian Availability
This matters more than you'd think. The Fluval Flex and Aqua One Focus are available at virtually every Petbarn and independent fish shop in the country. The Dennerle, UNS, and Waterbox require specialist retailers or online ordering — which means higher shipping costs and potentially longer waits for warranty replacements.
